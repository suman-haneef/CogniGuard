COGNIGUARD VERIFICATION KNOWLEDGE BASE

General Misinformation Guidelines

1. Extraordinary claims require strong evidence.
Claims that promise impossible, universal, or guaranteed results should be treated cautiously and verified with reliable evidence.

2. Absolute language can be a warning signal.
Words such as "always", "never", "100%", "completely", "guaranteed", and "cures every disease" can indicate exaggerated claims. However, the presence of these words alone does not prove that a claim is false.

3. Reliable verification should use credible sources.
For scientific claims, useful sources may include peer-reviewed research, recognized scientific institutions, universities, government health agencies, and established medical organizations.

4. Sources should be evaluated carefully.
Consider the author's identity, publication date, evidence provided, supporting references, and whether independent reliable sources report the same information.

5. Headlines can be misleading.
A headline may exaggerate or simplify the information contained in the original article. The full article and original source should be checked before reaching a conclusion.

6. Lack of evidence is not automatically proof of falsity.
If a claim has no supporting evidence available, CogniGuard should recommend verification rather than automatically declaring the claim false.

7. AI analysis has limitations.
An AI model can identify language patterns, inconsistencies, and potential warning signals, but it should not be treated as a final authority on factual truth.

8. Deepfake-related warning signals.
For suspected manipulated media, users should consider unusual facial movements, inconsistent lighting, unnatural audio, lip-sync problems, visual artifacts, and the reliability of the original source. These signals alone do not conclusively prove that media is manipulated.

9. Health claims require extra caution.
Claims about medicines, treatments, diseases, or medical discoveries should be verified using authoritative medical and scientific sources.

10. Political and public-interest claims require source verification.
Claims about public officials, elections, laws, or political events should be checked against reliable primary sources and reputable reporting rather than relying on viral posts alone.
